## Pros

- Experimentally used for hot air balloons, but due to heat sensitivity, not very useful with fire/heat caused lift
- Micro-fibrous and very low permeability
- Incredibly durable and tear resistant
- Relatively Cheap

## Cons

- Fusing via the usage of heat alone cause [bad seals](https://www.dupont.com/packaging-materials-and-solutions/seaming-and-sealing.html)
    - Need to use a method of sealing that does not involve heat (Hot glue guns, heat sealers, chemical heat glue)
- This material is more on the heavy side, weighing in at about 70 grams per square foot

## Weight

Tyvek weighs in at about 68 grams per square foot

  

## Can it Balloon?

At first, I was a bit nervous after reading that Tyvek’s purpose to keep [something in and others out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyvek). However, after looking more closely, the material shows a very promising result :

- [Helium Dispersion Through HDPE](https://www.headfullofair.com/2010/06/25/helium-dispersion-through-hdpe/)
    - Does not actually work with Tyvek specifically, however, Tyvek is made of 100% recycled HDPE, so if Tyvek ends up too porous, there is always non-tinted (metal oxide) HDPE
- [Tyvek Carryall-Envelope via FedEx](https://improvisedlife.com/2015/01/05/tyvek-carryall-envelope-via-fedex/)
    - Fedex envelopes are made of Tyvek
- [Bamboo/Tyvek Kite and Solar Balloon Construction workshop](https://publiclab.org/wiki/bambootyvek-kite-and-solar-balloon-construction-workshop)
